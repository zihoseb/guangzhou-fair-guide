Common Scams at Canton Fair You Must Know

📅 May 12, 2026 ⏱️ 8 min read 👁️ 3,247 views

The Canton Fair is amazing, but like any large international event, it attracts its share of scammers. Being informed is your best defense. Here are the most common scams reported by attendees and how to protect yourself.

⚠️ Remember This

If an offer seems too good to be true, it probably is. Always verify through official channels!

Scam 1: The Fake Buyer

How It Works:

Someone approaches your booth claiming to be a big buyer from your target market. They show fake credentials, express massive interest in your products, and suggest a private meeting outside the fairgrounds.

🚩 Red Flags:
  • Won't scan your badge or give proper contact info
  • Rushes you to make a decision immediately
  • Insists on meeting at a "private office" or restaurant
  • Asks for upfront payments for "customs" or "inspection"

Protection: Always meet at the fairground first. Verify their company through independent sources. Never meet alone in a private location without telling someone.

Scam 2: The Fake Translator

How It Works:

Someone offers translation services at very low prices, or even for free. Once hired, they intentionally mistranslate conversations to create conflict, then offer to "help" resolve issues for a fee.

🚩 Red Flags:
  • Price is significantly below market rate
  • No official badge or company affiliation
  • Suggests "simplifying" your message
  • Refuses to let you see translations in writing

Protection: Use the fair's official translation service or hire from reputable agencies with a physical office. Always get agreements in writing.

Scam 3: Fake Hotel Booking

How It Works:

You receive an email or call saying the hotel you booked is overbooked or closed. They offer a "better deal" at a partner hotel and ask for immediate payment to secure it.

🚩 Red Flags:
  • Email comes from a strange domain
  • Asks for payment via Western Union or Bitcoin
  • Refuses to connect you with the actual hotel
  • Creates urgency ("last room available!")

Protection: Always call the hotel directly to verify. Never click links in unsolicited emails. Book through major platforms you trust.

Scam 4: The Supplier Switch

How It Works:

You meet a great supplier at the fair, agree on prices and terms. After the fair, the "same supplier" emails you with bank details that don't match, or sends completely different products.

🚩 Red Flags:
  • Sudden change in contact person
  • Bank account details different from what was agreed
  • Email address doesn't match the business card
  • Refuses video calls or factory verification

Protection: Always verify account changes through multiple channels. Use written contracts with payment terms in place. Consider third-party payment protection services.

Security Checklist

  1. Keep your valuables in the hotel safe when not needed
  2. Don't leave bags unattended at the fair
  3. Take photos of business cards and catalogs immediately
  4. Use the official fair Wi-Fi, not open hotspots
  5. Keep digital copies of all important documents
  6. Share your schedule with someone back home
🔒 Report Immediately If You Get Scammed

Contact the fair police (they have an office at the venue) or call 110 for emergency police. Report incidents to your embassy too if needed.
