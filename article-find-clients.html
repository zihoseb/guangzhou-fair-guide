How to Find Clients at Canton Fair? 3 Scripts

📅 May 13, 2026 ⏱️ 10 min read 👁️ 1,923 views

Networking is the heart of the Canton Fair. Walking around and collecting business cards isn't enough - you need to have genuine, strategic conversations. Here are 3 proven scripts that work in real scenarios.

💡 Before You Start

Research the exhibitors beforehand. Know who you want to talk to, what they sell, and what makes them unique. Preparation makes conversations flow naturally!

Script 1: The Product-First Approach

Best for when you're genuinely interested in a supplier's products. Start with curiosity, not sales:

Conversation Starter:

You: "Hi, I noticed your [product name] - the design looks really impressive! I'm [your name] from [your country/company]. Can you tell me more about the materials you use?"

This works because:

  • You lead with a genuine compliment
  • You show you've paid attention to their product
  • You ask an open-ended question

How to Deepen the Conversation:

  1. Ask about minimum order quantities (MOQ)
  2. Enquire about production lead times
  3. Discuss customization options
  4. Ask about their export experience to your region

Script 2: The Problem-Solver Approach

Position yourself as someone who can help them solve problems, not just buy from them:

Conversation Starter:

You: "Hi, I'm [your name]. I've been working with buyers in [your industry] for [X] years. What's currently the biggest challenge for your business in reaching international markets?"

This shifts the dynamic from "buyer-seller" to "business partners." People love talking about their challenges!

✅ Pro Move

Listen more than you talk. Take notes on their challenges, then follow up with how you might help address them after the fair.

Script 3: The Referral Approach

Use social proof by mentioning a common connection or shared experience:

Conversation Starter:

You: "Hi! I was talking to [mutual contact] from [company] earlier, and they recommended I check out your booth. I'm [your name] - what do you specialize in here?"

Even if you don't have a mutual contact, you can reference the fair itself:

  • "I've been to the fair [X] times..."
  • "I was looking at the [product category] section and noticed you..."
  • "Someone told me this is the best booth for..."

Key Networking Principles

  1. Be memorable: Share one interesting fact about yourself or your business
  2. Collect contact info strategically: Note something specific about the person on their business card
  3. Follow up quickly: Email within 48 hours while you're still fresh in their mind
  4. Add value first: Don't ask for business immediately
  5. Stay organized: Use apps like CamCard to scan and organize business cards
